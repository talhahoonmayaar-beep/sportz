About buildingmaterialcalculator.netlify.app

Free material calculators for American homeowners and weekend builders — with the math shown, not hidden.

Why we built this

Every home project starts with the same question at the supplier counter: how much do I actually need? Order short and the job stalls mid-weekend; order long and half a pallet of sod or a ton of gravel sits in the driveway until it's worthless. The calculators that existed when we looked fell into two camps — stripped-down boxes that spat out a number with no context, or vendor tools built to upsell.

buildingmaterialcalculator.netlify.app takes a third path: each calculator shows the formula, the assumptions and the waste allowances right on the page, so you can check the result against a tape measure and a phone calculator. If our number and your number disagree, you should be able to see exactly why.

How our estimates are built

  • Units US suppliers use. Cubic yards and US tons for stone, cubic feet and yards for mulch, gallons for paint, linear panels for fence, pallets of 400–500 sq ft for sod.
  • Typical bulk densities. Crushed stone at 1.4 tons per yard, pea gravel at 1.45, river rock at 1.3 — the numbers quarries actually publish, rounded conservatively.
  • Waste as a choice, not a fudge. Compaction and cutting waste are configurable on every tool, defaulting to the values contractors use.
  • Costs stay optional. Enter a local price and get an estimate; leave it blank and get quantities only. We don't mark anything up.

What our calculators are not

They are planning tools, not engineering. They don't know your soil, the slope of your yard, whether your walls are plumb, or what your local code requires for fence posts near a property line. For structural, drainage, electrical or code-sensitive work, treat our numbers as a starting point and confirm with your supplier and local requirements. That caveat is on every page on purpose.

The site itself

buildingmaterialcalculator.netlify.app is free, supported by unobtrusive advertising, and requires no account. There's no app to install, nothing to sign up for, and no email gate in front of a calculator. If something computes wrong or a tool you need is missing, tell us — corrections and requests both get read.
