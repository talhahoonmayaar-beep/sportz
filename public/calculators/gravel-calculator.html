How the gravel calculation works

Gravel is sold by volume (cubic yards) or by weight (US tons), and the two connect through a material's bulk density. The math chain is always the same: work out the area, multiply by depth to get volume, convert to cubic yards by dividing by 27, then multiply by density to get tons.

Step 1 — volume in cubic feet: length × width × depth. A 24 × 12 ft driveway at 4 inches (0.333 ft) deep is 24 × 12 × 0.333 = 96 ft³. Circular areas use π × radius² × depth, and triangular beds use ½ × base × height × depth.

Step 2 — convert to cubic yards: divide by 27 because one cubic yard is 3 × 3 × 3 feet. Our example driveway is 96 ÷ 27 = 3.56 yd³.

Step 3 — convert to tons: multiply by the density of your material. Crushed stone runs about 1.4 tons per cubic yard, so 3.56 × 1.4 = 4.98 tons. Add the waste allowance — 10% is standard for driveways — and you should plan for roughly 5.5 tons.

Rule of thumb
One cubic yard of gravel covers about 80 sq ft at 4 inches deep, or 160 sq ft at 2 inches deep. One ton covers roughly 50 sq ft at 4 inches.

How to measure your project area

Rectangles and squares: measure length and width at the widest points — gravel edges rarely run straight, and rounding up is cheaper than a second delivery. Circles: measure the diameter across the center and let the calculator use the circle area formula. Irregular beds: sketch the shape, split it into rectangles and triangles, calculate each piece and add the totals. Slopes: measure along the slope surface, not the horizontal projection, because gravel follows the grade.

Depth is where most DIY estimates go wrong. Four inches sounds trivial until you realize a 24 × 12 ft driveway at 4 inches is a full 3.5 cubic yards — nearly two pickup loads. Measure the finished compacted depth you want, not the loose depth you dump, and let the waste percentage absorb the difference.

Gravel calculator FAQ

How much does a cubic yard of gravel weigh?

A cubic yard of gravel weighs roughly 2,800 pounds — about 1.4 US tons. Crushed stone runs 1.4–1.5 tons per yard, river rock about 1.3 tons, and rain-soaked gravel can weigh 10–15% more than dry, which matters when suppliers charge by weight.

How deep should gravel be for a driveway?

At least 4 inches compacted; 6–8 inches is better for daily parking or soft soils. Contractors build in two lifts — a coarse crushed base, then a 2–3 inch finish layer — which locks up far better than a single thick dump.

How much area does a ton of gravel cover?

Roughly 100 sq ft at 2 inches deep, 50 sq ft at 4 inches, or 33 sq ft at 6 inches. Angular crushed stone spreads thinner than rounded pea gravel, which rolls and settles deeper.

Should I order gravel by the yard or by the ton?

Match the unit your supplier quotes. Most quarries sell crushed stone by the ton and decorative stone by the yard or bucket — the calculator shows both so you never have to convert under pressure at the counter.

How much extra should I add for compaction?

10% for driveways and walkways, 5% for decorative beds on firm ground. Crushed stone with fines compacts 10–20% when tamped, so skipping the allowance is how driveways end up an inch short in year one.
