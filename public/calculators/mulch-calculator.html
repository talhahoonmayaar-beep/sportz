Mulch Calculator

Estimate how much mulch you need for garden beds and landscaping — in cubic yards for bulk delivery or bags for a car-boot run. Set your depth, add waste and price both options.

Calculate mulch for your beds

Bulk mulch is usually cheaper above ~2 cubic yards; bags win for small beds and easy storage. Double-mulched beds may only need a 1-inch top-up.

How the mulch calculation works

Mulch is a volume calculation with one extra conversion. Work out the bed area in square feet, multiply by depth in inches divided by 12 to get cubic feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards — or by your bag size for a bag count.

The formula: cubic feet = (length × width × depth in inches) ÷ 12. For a 20 × 4 ft bed at 3 inches deep: 20 × 4 × 3 ÷ 12 = 20 ft³, which is 20 ÷ 27 = 0.74 yd³ — or 10 of the standard 2-cubic-foot bags. Round up: ten bags today beats a return trip for one bag next weekend.

Why depth matters more than people expect: doubling depth doubles the order. The difference between 2 and 4 inches across a 300 sq ft bed is a full cubic yard — and the 4-inch version usually does your plants no favors.

Rule of thumb
One cubic yard of mulch covers about 160 sq ft at 2 inches deep, 108 sq ft at 3 inches, or 80 sq ft at 4 inches.

Choosing a mulch depth that actually helps plants

Deeper is not better. Above about 4 inches, mulch turns into a sponge that sheds water, rots crowns and harbors voles — the classic mulch volcano against tree trunks is the most common mulching mistake in the US. Keep mulch pulled back a few inches from trunks and stems so the root flare can breathe. In shaded, cool beds 2 inches is plenty; sun-baked southern exposures and new plantings justify 3.

Measure existing mulch before ordering a top-up. If last year's layer has compacted to an inch, you only need 1–2 inches of fresh material, which can halve the order. Rake the old layer loose before spreading so water still penetrates instead of sheeting off a matted surface.

Bulk vs bagged mulch: the real math

Bagged mulch at big-box prices typically runs $3–$6 for 2 cubic feet — the equivalent of $40–$80 per cubic yard. Bulk double-shredded hardwood delivered locally often lands between $30 and $50 per yard plus delivery. That is why the break-even sits near 1.5–2 cubic yards: a small 12 × 8 ft bed is easy in bags from the trunk, while a full foundation-planting refresh in bags means twenty-plus lifts from the parking lot and a trunk full of loose bark.

Bulk also means choosing the actual pile — ask for double- or triple-shredded hardwood for beds, verify it is not fresh-unground (it robs nitrogen as it decomposes), and check the pile center is not steaming-hot from composting. Dyed mulches hold color longer but keep them off vegetable gardens and young plants.

How to measure odd-shaped beds

Rings around trees: use the circle mode with the diameter of the mulched area. L-shaped beds: split into two rectangles, run the calculator twice, add the totals. Curved borders: measure the average width at three points and multiply by bed length — or split into rectangles and triangles. When in doubt between two measurements, use the larger one; mulch keeps and a short order means a second trip.

Mulch calculator FAQ

How many bags of mulch are in a cubic yard?

One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet: 13.5 two-cubic-foot bags, 18 one-and-a-half-cubic-foot bags, or 9 three-cubic-foot bags. Beyond about 2 cubic yards, bulk delivery almost always beats the per-bag price.

How deep should mulch be?

2–3 inches for most beds. Two inches suppresses weeds and holds moisture; three inches suits new plantings and hot climates. Never exceed four inches — deep mulch sheds water, smothers roots and rots stems kept in constant contact.

How much does a cubic yard of mulch weigh?

Dry shredded bark runs roughly 400–500 pounds per cubic yard; rain-wet mulch can double that. A half-ton pickup should carry no more than about 1.5 yards dry — bulk delivery exists for a reason.

How much area does a bag of mulch cover?

A 2-cubic-foot bag covers 12 sq ft at 2 inches deep or 8 sq ft at 3 inches; a 3-cubic-foot bag covers 18 sq ft at 2 inches or 12 sq ft at 3 inches. The calculator converts your order to bags automatically once you pick a size.

Should I buy mulch in bags or bulk?

Under ~1.5 cubic yards, bags: they store, stack and travel in a car trunk. Above ~2 cubic yards, bulk delivery is cheaper per volume and saves hours of hauling. Put your local bulk price into the price field and compare against the bag count.

How often should mulch be replenished?

Once a year in spring for shredded bark and wood chips. Rake the old layer loose, measure what remains, and top up to 2–3 inches total rather than dumping a fresh 3 inches on top of last year's matted layer.
