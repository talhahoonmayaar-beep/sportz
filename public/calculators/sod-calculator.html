Sod Calculator

Enter your lawn dimensions and get the pallets and rolls to order — cutting waste included. Sod is perishable, so the order needs to be right the first time.

Calculate sod for your lawn

Rolls are ~2 × 5 ft (10 sq ft). Sod must be laid within 24–48 hours of harvest — order for delivery the day your soil prep finishes, not before.

How the sod calculation works

Sod is the simplest volume-free calculation on the site: everything is square footage. Multiply length by width, add a waste allowance for cuts and curves, then divide by the pallet size your farm sells.

Step 1 — lawn area: length × width. A 50 × 100 ft yard is 5,000 sq ft — but measure the actual turf area, minus the house footprint, drives and beds, not the whole lot.

Step 2 — add waste: straight-edged rectangles waste almost nothing; curved beds and tree islands generate off-cuts. Five percent covers simple shapes, 10% handles most real yards with curves and obstacles.

Step 3 — divide by pallet size: 5,000 sq ft × 1.05 ÷ 450 sq ft per pallet = 11.7 → 12 pallets. Never round down: a half-pallet short means a visible seam running through next year's lawn, and no farm will re-open a field for one pallet.

Rule of thumb
450 sq ft is the most common pallet size. One pallet = 45 rolls of 10 sq ft each. A two-car driveway is about 800 sq ft for scale.

Measuring a yard that isn't a rectangle

Almost no one sods a perfect rectangle, so break the yard into shapes the calculator handles: main rectangle + side yard strips + circles for tree rings and round beds. Run each through the calculator and sum the pallets — the tool gives you square footage to order, so adding areas is safe. Subtract structures first: patios, sheds, drives and existing beds come out of the total before you order. Sketching the yard on graph paper with a tape measure is the twenty minutes that saves a $300 mis-order.

Two measurement traps catch homeowners every season: measuring the lot instead of the lawn (a quarter-acre lot rarely has a quarter-acre of turf), and forgetting that sod sold "by the roll" at garden centers costs far more per square foot than farm-direct pallets — fine for a 40 sq ft patch, expensive beyond that.

Before the pallets arrive

Sod keeps for 24–48 hours at best, so preparation has to be finished before delivery day: kill or strip the old lawn, rototill 4–6 inches, grade away from the foundation, and apply a starter fertilizer. Have the sprinkler working the day of install — new sod needs an inch of water within hours of unrolling and daily soaking for the first two weeks. Each pallet unrolls in 45–60 minutes for one worker; a 12-pallet lawn is a two-day job for a crew of two, so plan helpers or an installer accordingly.

Sod calculator FAQ

How much does a pallet of sod cover?

400–500 square feet, with 450 sq ft the most common US pallet size — about 45 rolls of 10 sq ft each. Always confirm the farm's pallet size before ordering; assuming 500 when they cut 400 leaves you a full pallet short.

How many pallets of sod do I need for my lawn?

Lawn square footage times 1.05–1.10 for waste, divided by pallet size, rounded up. A 5,000 sq ft lawn at 450 sq ft pallets is 12 pallets. Never round down — seams can't be fixed later.

How much does a pallet of sod weigh?

Roughly 1,500–3,000 pounds depending on moisture — plan around 2,000–2,500 pounds. That's trailer territory, not a half-ton pickup bed. Farms deliver or rent trailers for good reason.

How long can sod sit on a pallet?

24–48 hours maximum, same-day in summer heat. Stacked rolls heat up and yellow from the center outward. Lay first, water immediately, and keep unopened pallets in shade.

How much does sod cost per square foot?

Farm-direct sod runs $0.30–$0.80 per sq ft; delivered and installed runs $0.85–$1.50. A 5,000 sq ft lawn is roughly $1,500–$4,000 in material alone.

When is the best time to lay sod?

Cool-season grasses: early fall or spring. Warm-season grasses: late spring through summer. Sod goes down any time the ground isn't frozen — the real variable is how much irrigation the establishment window demands.
